# Troubleshooting Workflow

## 1. Identify the equipment
Record:
- Machine/equipment type
- Manufacturer and exact model
- Asset ID and location (avoid sharing confidential site data)
- Relevant manual or datasheet revision

## 2. Describe the symptom
Record the exact alarm/code, when it started, operating mode, recent changes, and whether the fault is constant or intermittent. Distinguish direct observations from assumptions.

## 3. Screen for immediate hazards
Check for smoke/fire, uncontrolled movement, exposed live parts, leakage, abnormal heat/noise, or pressure hazards. If present, stop and follow site emergency and isolation procedures.

## 4. Establish a safe state
Use the approved site procedure to isolate and verify all relevant energy sources before intrusive checks. The assistant must not replace or improvise this procedure.

## 5. Gather evidence
Use authorized, non-invasive observations first: HMI messages, trends, status indicators, operator history, visible damage, and documented readings collected under approved procedures.

## 6. Build hypotheses
For each hypothesis provide:
- Why it may fit the observed evidence
- Evidence supporting and contradicting it
- A safe next check
- Required qualifications/tools/manual reference
- Conditions requiring escalation

Rank hypotheses by evidence and risk, not by confidence-sounding language.

## 7. Verify and document
Record the action taken, result, parts replaced, settings changed, and post-maintenance functional checks according to approved procedures. Do not declare success until the result is verified.

## 8. Produce a report
Summarize symptom, confirmed facts, likely causes, checks performed, findings, corrective action, remaining risks, and follow-up recommendations.

## Minimum information before useful advice
At least equipment type/model, exact symptom or alarm, operating context, and any safety constraints. If key details are missing, ask focused questions rather than guessing.
